# Known limits

<sub>[← README](../README.md) · [all documentation](../README.md#documentation)</sub>

- Results are single small runs, apart from the benchmark's 4 to 10 rounds.
- A character's memories don't age yet: a lesson stays until forgotten by hand (`memory forget`), however old or wrong. Jev's pick keeps the prompt bounded, not the store. The offline replay that measured the memories' effect is 98 moments of one character on one goal, and the long soak that compared memory with none is one run each, in a hostile soak world, before the road-leave stall was fixed ([results](results.md#long-range-memory-over-a-long-soak)).
- Talk is for the local server only: NPCs hear the fleet's characters by name, so a player who took one of those names would be heard too. NPCs talk only when they meet; in a 20-minute run of three spread-out NPCs that was 3 lines.
- After a death, the corpse is taken back by opening it, which re-equips on AOS-era servers; the pre-AOS path (taking each item, `rewear`) hasn't run live. A corpse lying where danger was seen within 15 minutes (its region, or in the wilds within about 50 tiles) is left until `go_anyway`, and corpses decay in the meantime; by a spawner the danger is always there.
- Against six melee monsters at once a mage now wins 4 rounds in 10 (`swarm`), up from none, but on the final code with 5 deaths in 10; the rules win at most one. On 2026-10-09 it won 2 to 6 rounds in 10 depending on the lane and run, with one client or four at once. Jev is offered summons, fields and curses but hardly ever picks them.
- Necromancy and chivalry are tested only under the local server's AOS rules. ClassicUO's chivalry table has OSI's mana costs (Divine Fury 10 where ModernUO asks 15), used only for the client's own check before casting. The agent can't restock tithing points.
- Plans for fights, the planner's or hand-written, haven't beaten Jev with no plan on any scenario tried (`swarm`, `attrition`, the two packs), and haven't been soak-tested in sessions.
- A pack's weight comes from the store's creature stats; a creature it doesn't know counts 0.5.
- Bandage timing and spell failures are read by cliloc number where the server sends one (ModernUO does, under both rule sets), and from the English text otherwise. They have not yet been checked against what UO Renaissance itself sends.
- The two RunUO line-of-sight rules (`agent_sight_rule`: `runuo`, `runuo22`) are unit-tested against RunUO's source, not checked against a live RunUO server; only `modernuo` has been compared with a server's answers. UO Renaissance's own changes to the rule aren't known.
- On pre-AOS shards, item names take a few seconds to learn (one single click each), so the first loot judgment on a corpse can see tile names.
- To read a spellbook or a bag, the agent opens it once, so its gump flashes briefly.
- Archers don't pick up arrows that missed and fell on the ground (before Samurai Empire servers drop them there; from it on they come back on their own once the archer stops fighting).
- A tamer knows its pet only while it's in sight: a pet left behind out of sight is lost to the agent even if it lives. Taming new pets isn't done.
- The client can't see whom a pet is actually fighting, only whom it was told to: a pet that switched to another attacker still shows its order.
- Bards don't use peacemaking on themselves (calming everyone), and songs aren't scored for difficulty: Jev only knows a song "can fail, more often against strong creatures".
- Map markers: only UOAM `.map` and the client's CSV are read, not Ultima Mapper's `.xml` (which ClassicUO also loads). A `.map` file that a shard wrote with the client's map indexes rather than UOAM's facet numbers needs `--facets client`; nothing in the file tells them apart. UO Renaissance's marker files haven't been found yet: its website was down on 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09 (cuo-49t.8).
- Underground areas such as the Britain sewer can't be walked to (a dungeon's floor is a part of the map apart from the land, and travel can't use the entrances yet). `hunting_spots` and `what_spawns` now say so (`reach`: "not on foot ...") and list them after every place a walk reaches (cuo-432); before that the planner learned it only by failing, 14 times of 14 in the logs.
- Travel's memory of doors, locked doors and houses lasts while the client runs; it isn't saved. A door is judged locked when it hasn't moved after two tries, so one refused for another reason counts as locked for the session too.
- A long trip to a place walking can't reach, when the goal isn't closed off within 96 tiles of it (a large island), walks hop by hop until a hop brings it no nearer, then stops with "no route".
- A walk pace needs the profile's Always Run option off (it is off unless the player turned it on): with it on, the client runs every step. Mounted paces haven't been tried.
- A follower whose target is in view with no way to it (walled in, across water) stays `following` with a `why`, and looks for a way again every 8 seconds; each look that fails holds the game thread for about 0.15 s (measured in open ground, 2026-10-10). Four followers at once have followed one leader in the party runs (walking together, closing up before a hunt), with nothing measured about which tiles they took. The follow doesn't know which way its target faces, so "beside" is whichever free tile is nearest.
- Houses and rows of houses were tried on the local server only, placed by a staff character (a single house, a tower, and rows of seven, solid or with one-tile alleys); UO Renaissance's countryside hasn't been walked.
- The walk from Britain to Cove is about 1,500 tiles and 6 to 9 minutes, round the north of the mountains west of Cove. Under Jev's defend loop a lone warrior got there in five walks out of five (after the stall fix), with lowest health between 5% and 54%: ettins, ogres and a bog thing are on the way. Recall is the way to Cove when a rune or runebook entry for it is carried.
- A leave that Jev chooses, rather than the pack rule, runs from the nearest creature only, which can be towards the rest (cuo-d28.11).
- Where it runs ([more](playing.md#where-it-runs)): Jev's places lost to code's line away in two benches (43/60 and 44/60 against 47/60, with 12 and 10 deaths against 3 and 4), and code's pick of the same places tied with it (48/60 against 49/60), so places are off by default ([why](results.md#where-it-runs)). The places come from the planner's grid (map files, doors, houses, items in view); the client's own pathfinder can disagree, and then the run falls back to the line away. Whether a walk passes next to a creature is judged on the shortest walk found, which may not be the one the pathfinder takes. Moving and acting in one decision (a mage casting while it walks to a doorway) isn't done (cuo-fhf.4).
- Unattended, a lone warrior now leaves packs and lived through an hour with two sent at it, but it pays in time: after leaving, the planner tends to wait out the 30-minute refusal in town rather than hunt elsewhere (31.7 of 56 minutes resting in that run). The 30 tiles and 30 minutes are as first set.
- Jev's routine hunt calls have run live only in the soak runs (2026-10-07); the yes and no thresholds (0.65, 0.35) are as first set. In a thinly spawned area most hunts end on "unsure twice running" after about a minute, which hands back to the planner often.
- Sparing Jev calls (cuo-df8): the 8 s longest wait while quiet, the 7 tiles that end the quiet and the minute an answer is used again are as first set. Beside other goals, creatures within 7 tiles still make calls, so walking through a town with animals about costs 4-21 calls a minute.
- A headless client still opens its graphics device and loads the game files, so it takes about half a windowed client's memory, not next to none, and it needs a logged-in macOS session like any other.
- Outside a fleet, nothing logs an agent in again after a lost connection: the client waits at the login screen until `uo-brain login` or `client` logs it in. A fleet does it itself ([several agents on one server](fleets.md#several-agents-on-one-server)).
- Line of sight reads what the client has of the map: an item it hasn't been sent can't block in its rule.
- Only an archer or a caster engaged at range walks round to see its creature. A bard sings only at what it can see, and a tamer's pet order at a creature out of sight is refused by the server; neither walks to get sight, so with nothing in sight they wait.
- The sight check around Britain looked from where `[AgentGo` put the character, on the land; targets on upper floors came up only where a floor was the standing place picked.
- World facts for fights are shortlisted by area and keyword: a fact stored under another area name, or that names a creature differently, can't be picked. Jev's picks trail putting every shortlisted fact in (8/10 against 10/10 in the wisp scenario).
- A party has been measured on two scenes, six runs a condition at most ([results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)): the lich scene (a lich, two bone knights and four skeletons at Britain's graveyard, below) and, since 2026-10-14, casters and archers from two sides (three skeletal mages and two skeletons, and two ratman archers and a bone magus 18 tiles east of them). On the second, formed up nobody died in six runs, against five deaths each on the defaults and walking together (the mage in four of six runs on each), and walking together didn't keep the party together in the fight as it does on the lich scene; its rally point was where the leader stopped, so it says nothing of `rally_map`. Both scenes are at one graveyard, in the open; a corridor or a dungeon level, where the rally point is the way out, hasn't been staged. On the lich scene: On the defaults its members reach the fight 9 to 11 tiles from their leader and scatter: in each of six runs somebody stood apart from all the others for 17 to 50 of the first 60 seconds, and four members died. Two settings keep it together, and both are off unless a fleet file asks: `walk` (one death in six runs) and `form_up` (three deaths in fifteen runs: none in the first seven, the tamer twice and the warrior once in the eight on the reviewed code, and nobody apart in twelve of the fifteen). The code of `form_up` changed between those runs, five versions in a night, each for what the last had shown, so its runs are not one sample; the last version has seven, and one fix is later than any run (one with nothing to fight with isn't held beside what is on it). Formed up, the party fights within a few tiles of its rally point (the warrior 1 to 8 tiles from it in the five runs looked at), so falling back from the line takes a member a few steps at most. From the third version on the wave lands on the warrior, as it is meant to, and he died in three of fifteen such fights (four of them the fights of staged checks), each time from over half his health in about a second. Callouts changed nothing six runs can show: not deaths, and not the lich's time to die.
- A party's leader goes back for its corpse with its companions only when the fight was won (companions at the place, or beside it with nobody driven off). The last part of that rule was written after the runs that showed the need and is unit-tested, not run live.
- A party that walks its trips sets out for a fight at a walk and runs once danger is in view. At six runs each on the lich scene it called a leave far less often than a party whose members make their own way (3 against 28) and lost one member against four; three runs of 2026-10-11, with Jev in the fights, had said the opposite. One scene, and a short trip to it.
- The huddle's planners agree a plan in words and then each sets out when its own planner says. Formed up, the first at the edge waits for those who have said they are on their way, so those who come are in the fight within seconds of each other (6.4, 4.5 and 15.4 s in three runs with memory on, against half a minute before), but it doesn't make five planners choose the hunt: one or two never came in every run, and one whose planner chose the hunt ten seconds after the first had gone in wasn't waited for ([results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)). Those runs are from before the plan named who goes: the first at the edge now also waits for those the plan sends, and one who can't take blows waits up to 45 s for a fighter who is coming ([how](fleets.md#a-party-in-a-fight-on-the-road-and-at-the-inn)). Played live in six runs on the lich scene with memory on (three as built, three after the fix below), it didn't make the members come: their planners rested (18 to 27 times a run) or walked to the healer while the plan sent them to the hunt, the lich died in one run of six, and someone was apart for most of the first minute in five ([results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)); the plan's list of who goes didn't bind a member's planner. Since 2026-10-19 the plan does: a member it names is sent into its hunt when system one reads the plan as sending the party in now, and kept from it while the plan holds back. With memory on, the plans held the party back in all three runs (enter only after a fresh scout), so nobody went in; with memory off all five were sent at once and were in within 5.0, 14.0 and 6.6 s, the ranged ones opening fire from the edge before the warrior reached the creatures ([results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)). What it leaves besides: a member the plan sends who is near, alive and not on an errand, and whose planner chose to rest, is waited for the whole 25 s (45 s for the party's only fighter) each time a rally is held anew, and then gone in without; a member whose own trip into the hunt stops at the rally point waits there for the word and then needs a planner call to go in, so it is in some seconds after the others; a walk the code doesn't stop (one that isn't its planner's trip, or one into a hunt the plan doesn't send it to) can still take a member past the rally, and the party then goes in after it unless a fighter is on his way; and the plan is tied to a hunt by the place's name being in its target line, so a plan that names the place another way is read as saying nothing of who goes. In one of the runs as built a mage's walk to the graveyard went past the rally and the warrior went in after her alone (two died); the trip that stops at the rally point came after that.
- Going in together ([how](fleets.md#a-party-in-a-fight-on-the-road-and-at-the-inn)): unless a fleet asks for `rally_map`, the rally point is where the leader stood when it reached the edge or first saw something hostile. With `rally_map` it is chosen for a way out behind it, with a second point further back, by the client's map of what can be walked to; on the lich scene it put the point 5 tiles back along the way in, with the second 12 tiles behind it, and the client answered for its map in 15 to 39 ms; in three runs against three without it nobody died either way and nobody ran to the second point, so whether a party loses fewer members with it is not known. It is not chosen for cover: nothing asks what blocks sight. A point is judged by ground alone, so a creature or a companion standing on it counts for nothing, and a door is never a way out. Heights aren't in the map the brain is given: two tiles side by side that the client can each walk to are taken to be joined, which a ledge between them would make wrong. A way out is judged within the 32 tiles of that map, so ground closed further off than that passes for open. One who fell back to the second point and whose party was then wiped hunts on from there (its run ends when nobody is left fighting, the same as when the others won), until an ordinary leave is decided. A hunt begun inside the area more than 40 tiles from the point the party went in from (45 with `rally_map`) gets a new rally point where the leader stands, in the spawn: in one run the second and third hunts rallied 9 tiles from the graveyard's centre. The line is held against creatures in war mode only, and a warrior holding it does nothing to a spellcaster that stands off until only spellcasters are left. Who is beside whom is what each client can see: a companion out of view counts for nothing in the line or the leg of a walk. A hunt carried on while the leader is down doesn't form up. Going to a companion that is set upon rests on what a member can see (a creature in war mode within 2 tiles of a companion in view) or on the companion's own word when it is out of view, which misses a chaser between its blows; a mage under 30% of her mana, a tamer whose pet is called back or under half its health, and anyone under half their own health don't go. It has gone to a companion out of view once in a live run unstaged (the bard, to the archer with a bone knight on him) and in one staged check of five (the warrior, the mage and the bard, all looting, to an archer put 24 tiles off and held there). And it is a rule of the fight, not of the trip: a member setting off on the party's next goal (its leader's resurrection, say) leaves a companion that still has something on it.
- Raising the fallen is said to work about one try in four at the test kit's skills (Healing and Anatomy 80, Magery 90); in its two live runs 2 tries of 16 did ([results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)). Only a warrior's bandages or a mage's Resurrection do it: the other three of the lich scene's five can't. A ghost waits in war mode, which a real shard's players would see too. Both runs staged the death; a death the fight itself dealt hasn't been raised live. It is off unless a fleet asks for it (`raise = true`).
- A party for the player: no person has played what is described here since it was built (2026-10-13, late): every run had a script as the leader, saying the lines of Harry's playtest of that evening ([results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)). What the leader is fighting is whatever stands in war mode within 4 tiles of it, and beyond that what system one judges, creature by creature, to be its fight (cuo-cym.25): on 15 held-back creatures it took one walking away for the fight, and live it is one run each way, with a ratman archer that kept its distance. A creature the leader shoots that neither faces it nor comes nearer, and isn't wounded, can still be left alone; the question is asked at most every 3 s, while a creature in war mode stands beyond the rule (told "kill everything you see", a companion goes for what is hostile within 12 tiles). There are four orders (come along, wait, go somewhere and wait there, sit down), said as you like, and the reading isn't sure: on lines it hadn't been tried on the model read 92 of 102 as written. A line that points back at what was said is the weak place: "off you go then" is read with the talk before it, "yes, there" was read as talk, and "nearly there, save me a seat", said to companions waiting at the inn, as an order to go. Three orders for the fight itself are carried out, with creatures in view: go for the one you name ("kill the mage first", "get the one on me"), fall back to you, and heal someone now (cuo-cym.24). Two of the three have been tried live with a scripted leader (all three companions took "get the one on me" in one run and "fall back" in two; a tamer's pet fought on through the fall back), and heal someone now only on written lines: on 42 lines held back, Decision 1 read 112 of 126 as written (Jev 117), and what it got wrong it mostly got wrong safely, as no order: "help Thea with that ghoul" and "leave it, leave it!" were none, and the companion fought on. Jev once sent a companion at the wrong creature ("pull the near one": the deer nearer it, not the orc nearer you). An order for one companion by name can be taken by the others when they don't talk (who a line is for is read only in a party that talks): "patch me up Thea" was a heal for Isolde and Wystan as well. Only the six creatures nearest the companion are offered, so one further off can't be named; "follow me" in a fight doesn't pull a companion out of it (say "fall back"); a line read as an order to fight that names no creature in view gets "Which one?". The rest ("cover me", "hold them off", "res him now") aren't carried out: a companion that talks reads them as requests and says what it will and won't do, and one without talk says nothing. That reading isn't sure either: on lines it hadn't been tried on, 16 of 21 readings of a request and 24 of 27 of talk were right; "all guard me", said to your own pets, was a request to all three, a question can be taken for one ("any bandages left?" was; the answer is then told to answer the question), and an order with a fight in it can lose its order ("Come on, and kill anything that moves." was a request, and nobody came). An errand ("get me 10 bandages from the healer", "go bank this gold", "sell the loot") is carried out and the companion comes back (cuo-cym.27; one run live), but only buying, banking and selling, and only the goods it knows (bandages, potions, arrows, bolts, reagents as a restock); "fetch me a pickaxe" is read as being sent to wait, and it buys from the nearest shop that sells the thing, whoever was named ("from Bob"). Settings change at your word, and on unseen lines 134 of 156 readings were right: a remark or a request for one thing now can be taken for a rule ("I always go for the biggest one" set strongest first, "nice and safe, that one" cautious). Each change is said aloud, so say it the other way; a setting holds for the run and is not kept for the next. Sent somewhere, a companion goes to the nearest place of that kind the world store knows, or to a place named by its name that the store knows ("the east bank", "the healer in Yew"; cuo-cym.27); a place the store doesn't know by name is no option, and in a line with several places it picked its own in 3 of 4 held-back lines ("I'm off to the smith's, you lot head to the healer's" sent it to the smithy); it runs there by itself, not in formation with the others sent, and it may say no. Speech needs line of sight on ModernUO; a companion on the other side of a wall within earshot hears you through its brain, which reads your lines in your own client (cuo-cym.28), so that client has to be ours (with an agent port) and on this machine, and a line reaches it about 1.5 s late. One you have sent away beyond earshot hears only party chat. A line to all of them is answered by each, about 3 s apart, so every remark to the party can bring three lines; "less chatter" keeps them to answering what is put to them, which is still each of them. The first answer came about 6 s after the line in the live runs (5.6 to 8.7 s for lines of 30 to 50 characters; the wait is set at four fifths of the time a person takes to read and type, and came out at nine tenths, with some answers held back up to a second more by something not yet found; `pace = 0.7` in the fleet file shortens it further and `pace = false` says each line as soon as it is written), nobody answers in a fight, and each says at most 40 lines an hour, which three answers to every line use up sooner. Who a line is for is read by what each plays as ("our archer") and not by what a persona knows ("the bear's mother" is nobody). Companions don't restock, bank, buy or sell on their own (only on an errand you send them on), nor form up: the table is held where the leader says "sit down", inn or not, and its turns go round the companions only (the leader speaks when it likes). The leader is known by its name on first sight (local server).
- The game's own party: an invitation needs the member within 8 tiles of the leader and nothing on the leader, so a member that joins mid-fight waits for the fight to end. Party chat for callouts has run on the test server only.
- The talk, the table, the callouts and the gestures are for the local server only. Callouts are overhead speech unless a fleet says `callouts_to = "party"`. A table written by Haiku invents small things now and then (a count of bandages); the story of the hunt it is written from is gathered in code, and Sonnet kept to it.
- Which creatures only fight back (birds, deer, rabbits) comes from ModernUO's `FightMode`; a shard whose store wasn't imported from ModernUO (UO Renaissance's) doesn't know it, so there every grey creature still counts as a hostile in the planner's situation (cuo-s9p).
- A hamlet (cuo-m3n) is one run each way so far, an hour long, of four residents of Cove. Its residents' work is made of the goals a fighter has (hunting, buying, selling, banking), so a resident whose work is a hunting ground 120 tiles out spends most of the hour there (the hunter: a sixth of it in Cove); there is no inn or bank in Cove, no evening together, and nothing a resident does for another (the healer's apprentice doesn't tend the hurt). Gathering and crafting, which would make work at hand, are out of scope ([results](results.md#a-hamlet-of-ai-residents)).
- A creature with a person's shape is told from a person by its body (a savage's) or a title in its name ("the brigand"), as townsfolk are (cuo-ea4): a player with savage kin paint, or with a title in its name, would be taken for a monster and fought, and a human-shaped monster with neither (none is known on ModernUO) for a player. On a real shard the agent attacks no player; this is where that rests on a guess ([playing](playing.md)).
- Shops: a vendor is asked by its name, which rests on the server knowing its vendors by name (ModernUO does). Where it doesn't, the third ask is "vendor buy", which goes to whichever vendor stands nearest, as every ask did before ([why](goals.md#travel-banking-shops-and-hunting)). A vendor has to be stood next to for words, and in a shop one tile wide another townsperson can stand in the only way to it (on Felucca a character short of full stamina can't push past). Since 2026-10-19 an errand that can't get next to its vendor after two walks up asks by the vendor's context menu from up to 8 tiles (cuo-cgy); on a shard without context menus it still walks up six times and fails with "could not get next to the vendor" ([results](results.md#asking-a-vendor-by-name)).
- Steps the server sends back ([how](goals.md#travel-banking-shops-and-hunting)): a refused tile is left alone for 15 s whatever the reason, so a tile refused because someone hidden stood on it is gone round for that long after they leave, and a character that can't step anywhere (too tired to move, say) would find every tile it tried closed for 15 s afterwards; neither has been seen or tried. The walker reads a static by what the map says it is, but land is still read by the season's art, which differs for winter only (no map of the test server is in winter). The planner's own map takes a static's surface as the server does since 2026-10-19 (cuo-5y8, cuo-u4j): a stair stepped onto only from within 2 of its base and anything else from within 2 of its top, measured from the full top of what one stands on; the ground still from up to 8 below it, generous for slopes, since the client's pathfinder walks each leg exactly. Its check against the client's walker (`scripts/nav_check.py`) counts tiles behind a door as the grid's alone, since that walker doesn't open doors.
- Decision 1, the default for most of system one since 2026-10-12, has played the fight benchmark, the wisp scenario, soak hours and the party's lich scene (at least as well as Jev there in three runs each, at two thirds of the cost: [results](results.md#a-party-that-goes-in-together)), and no cuts are tuned for it. Its re-ranks, memory picks, recorder and chat calls are measured only by agreement with Jev.
- Jev deciding when a leave stops (`--leave-stop jev`, cuo-fhf.6) is off by default: in the bench it made leaves far shorter (9 s against 32 s) but three times as many, with the wins unchanged ([results](results.md#when-a-leave-stops)).
